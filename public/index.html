Credit monitoring shouldn’t mean searching through spreadsheets.

Every quarter, private credit and commercial banking analysts compare each borrower’s results with the limits in its own loan agreement: how much debt it may carry, how comfortably its cash flow covers its loan payments.

When the figures live in one file and the definitions in another, finding the borrowers that need attention, how far they are from compliance and which clause applies is slow, manual work.

From financial data to a clearer credit decision.

Covenant Monitor reads quarterly borrower figures and the loan policy, then answers three questions in order.

  1. 1

    Detect

    Identify the borrowers that need attention.

    Track each borrower every quarter against leverage, debt-service coverage, fixed-charge coverage and the current ratio; springing tests run only when the revolver is drawn past its trigger. A priority-ordered list puts covenant failures first, then results that cannot be calculated or verified, then borrowers nearing a limit.

From the term sheet to the client call.

The challenge asks for one of three covenant checks, with no interface required. Covenant Monitor runs all three, then follows the rest of the credit cycle. Cure terms, reporting dates, the covenant addendum and the statement PDFs are synthetic.

  1. 1

    Set the terms

    Fixed-charge coverage and the current ratio join leverage and debt-service coverage. Each covenant can be tested every quarter, only when the revolver is at least 35% drawn, or not at all, for the portfolio or a single borrower. The Covenant Designer reads a company’s statements as a CSV or a PDF and recommends a package with a cushion, a springing suggestion and draft term-sheet language for review. The fixed-charge, current-ratio and springing terms come from a team-written synthetic addendum.

    Read the statements

    Each line item in a borrower’s statements PDF comes back with its page and, where available, its quoted text. Our code calculates, the analyst confirms; nothing is auto-accepted. Some values have a page location but no quoted text, and those can be confirmed only through a passing tie-out. A separate check compares a compliance certificate’s certified ratios with the engine’s.

  4. 4

    Find the cure

    For a borrower in breach, an optimisation model proposes the cheapest cure the agreement allows: an equity cure, early repayment or an asset sale. The exact engine re-checks every plan in whole cents against all four covenants and shows a plan only if it passes. When no cure exists, the tool names the blocking terms and the smallest waiver. A plan or waiver is decision support, not a legal determination.

Built for decisions that require evidence.

Covenant Monitor is a hackathon prototype. These are the design choices that make each of its results checkable.

Deterministic calculations
Covenant ratios and dollar headroom are calculated in code, not generated by a language model.Exact whole-cent arithmetic; a ratio exactly at its limit passes. Optimisation proposals are re-checked by the exact engine before they are shown.
Agreement-based thresholds
Each covenant limit comes from the supplied fictional loan policy and the borrower’s data row.If a row’s threshold does not appear in the policy sentence, the tool says so.
Traceable evidence
Every flagged result links to its source record and the agreement definition behind it.Each quoted sentence carries its character position and a SHA-256 hash.
Transparent limitations
Missing or invalid inputs are flagged, never quietly treated as compliant.Unreadable rows, absent reports and uncalculable ratios are listed for review.

This demonstration uses fictional, synthetic financial data. Covenant exceptions are signals for analyst review and do not constitute legal default determinations.
