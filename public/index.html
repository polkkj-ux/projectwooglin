Credit monitoring shouldn’t mean searching through spreadsheets.

Every quarter, private credit and commercial banking analysts compare each borrower’s results with the limits in its own loan agreement: how much debt it may carry, how comfortably its cash flow covers its loan payments.

When the figures live in one file and the definitions in another, finding the borrowers that need attention, how far they are from compliance and which clause applies is slow, manual work.

From financial data to a clearer credit decision.

Covenant Monitor reads quarterly borrower figures and the loan policy, then answers three questions in order.

  1. 1

    Detect

    Identify the borrowers that need attention.

    Track each borrower against its covenant thresholds every quarter. A priority-ordered list puts covenant failures first, then results that cannot be calculated or verified, then borrowers nearing a limit.

    Know how far a borrower is from compliance.

    See each ratio against its limit, the dollar headroom or shortfall, and how the position moved since the previous quarter.

Built for decisions that require evidence.

Covenant Monitor is a hackathon prototype. These are the design choices that make each of its results checkable.

Deterministic calculations
Covenant ratios and dollar headroom are calculated in code, not generated by a language model.Exact whole-cent arithmetic; a ratio exactly at its limit passes.
Agreement-based thresholds
Each covenant limit comes from the supplied fictional loan policy and the borrower’s data row.If a row’s threshold does not appear in the policy sentence, the tool says so.
Traceable evidence
Every flagged result links to its source record and the agreement definition behind it.Each quoted sentence carries its character position and a SHA-256 hash.
Transparent limitations
Missing or invalid inputs are flagged, never quietly treated as compliant.Unreadable rows, absent reports and uncalculable ratios are listed for review.

This demonstration uses fictional, synthetic financial data. Covenant exceptions are signals for analyst review and do not constitute legal default determinations.
